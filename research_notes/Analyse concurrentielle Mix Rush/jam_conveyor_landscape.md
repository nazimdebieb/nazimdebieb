## Q1: Pixel Flow (Loom Games): launch, revenue/download milestones, rank history, team, core loop, features added since launch

### Takeaway
Loom Games (Istanbul) launched Pixel Flow! on 17 Aug 2025. It is the category-defining hit of 2025–2026, with about $120–125M+ in IAP (AppMagic) in its first ~11 months and $104M IAP in H1 2026 alone. That equals 23% of all hybrid-casual puzzle IAP. It became the only casual game launched in the prior 12 months to reach the US monthly top-20 grossing chart. In February 2026 Scopely bought a majority stake at a valuation above $1B. A team of about 10–20 people built it. The loop works as follows. The player taps limited-ammo colored "piggy" shooters onto a conveyor belt that circles a pixel-art picture, and each shooter destroys pixels of its own color. Shooters with ammo left return to a small bench, and a full bench means failure. The design is deterministic and skill/timing-based. Competitive 24-hour live ops (Pixel Arena, Fire Quest), a Pixel Pass, No-Ads and fail offers sit on top of it.

**Core loop (what exactly the player does)**
- Kidscreen's summary: players "control… a rotating conveyor belt where they must place colored piggies that shoot balls to break up pixel bricks and clear the level" — [Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/2026/02/19/scopely-plans-to-turn-mobile-hit-pixel-flow-into-a-franchise/)
- Mechanics listed by DoF:
  - Shooters have limited bullets and only clear blocks of their own color.
  - There is a conveyor belt with a rotating tray, and a bench where shooters return after firing.
  - Strict slot limits define the loss condition, and only the front row is interactable.
  - "Slinging" lets the player chain actions to stack up to 10 units.
  - "Double blocks that consume two slots" arrive later in progression.
  - The game is fully deterministic, with no RNG.
  - Source: [DoF](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- It is an iteration on Voodoo's This Is Blast! (soft launch Feb 2025, about $30K/day). Pixel Flow kept the shooting and added a conveyor around the picture, which increases the sorting area "4 times basically" — [Gamigion, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.gamigion.com/pixel-flow-by-loom-games-just-breached-through-300k-a-day/). DoF says This Is Blast "plateaued around $40K per day" — [DoF](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- A player can have "5 piggies around the picture and 4 more on the bench", and slinging creates "tension… very novel for a puzzler without a time based fail state" — [Gamigion, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/pixel-flow-by-loom-games-biggest-turkish-game-since-royal-kingdom/)
- The design sits between relaxed and timed puzzles. The conveyor keeps the board moving and "forces imperfect decisions", and failures feel self-inflicted. Each level "looks visually different" and gives a before/after payoff, so "every level is a new ad". Pixel-art nostalgia may attract "a slightly older audience" that monetizes better (analyst hypothesis) — [Gamigion/F. Mata](https://www.gamigion.com/why-is-pixel-flow-monetizing-so-well/)

**Monetization, progression and features (as observed Feb–Aug 2026)**
- FTUE and monetization timeline, per [Gamigion/J. Iljuk, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/pixel-flow-by-loom-games-is-making-over-500k-a-day/):
  - Level 1 onboards "in seconds".
  - The first event ("Lava Quest") is shown early but locked until level 70.
  - At level 10, a rewarded ad doubles the coin reward.
  - After a fail, the player can spend coins or buy a $6.99 fail offer.
  - From level 20, forced ads start and a No-Ads offer appears; the No-Ads push repeats at level 27.
  - At level 36, the Pixel Pass is introduced.
  - The shop is led by the $8.99 No-Ads bundle and the $9.99 Pixel Pass.
- Pixel Arena unlocks at level 31. It is a leaderboard with a streak multiplier: 1x, 2x, then 10x after 5+ consecutive clears. The Pixel Pass is force-onboarded at level 35 with 15 minutes of unlimited hearts, and level 36 is a "Very Hard" wall — [Gamigion/S. Kim, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/pixel-flows-lv36-trap-streaks-fomo-monetization-collide/)
- Fire Quest first appears at level 71 and rewards surviving 7 levels in a row. Every level pays 40 coins regardless of difficulty, and the cheapest item costs 900 coins. On average only 2.1 Normal levels sit between Hard/Very-Hard levels (83% of gaps are 3 levels or fewer) — [Gamigion/S. Kim, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/why-the-latecomers-could-not-replicate-pixel-flow/)
- Fire Quest and Pixel Arena run on 24-hour cycles with leaderboards. The game supports Google Play Games / Game Center sign-in and cloud save, and runs on a Nakama backend proven for 2M concurrent users. "One month old when the copycats started appearing" — [Heroic Labs, 4 Mar 2026](https://heroiclabs.com/blog/pixel-flow-highlight-announcement/)
- New elements are introduced at levels 7, 12, 14 ("Connected Pig") and 18 (Superpig power-up), each followed by a difficulty spike — [Gamigion/E. Eck, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/inside-pixel-flows-difficulty-curve/)
- The shop expands progressively: coins only until about level 20, then boosters and bundles, then No-Ads, and "a Season Pass slot will be added later". It has no free daily gift and no flash deals — [Gamigion/M. Khripin, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/whats-wrong-with-pixel-flows-game-shop/)
- DoF notes "no rewarded shortcuts bypassing difficulty", with interstitials on failure and rewarded ads only for extra lives — [DoF](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- Another analyst describes "35 levels of zero friction, then one precisely timed wall". The game is "fully deterministic, no RNG", and "engagement numbers are nearly 3x the category average" — [Gamigion/O. Fomina, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/pixel-flow-magic-sort-knit-out-why-players-can-not-stop/)

### Inferences
- Lifetime IAP through September 2026 can be estimated roughly. H1 2026 was about $104M and Aug–Dec 2025 was about $17M, which gives about $121M by end of June. If monthly IAP stayed near the Mar–Jun level of about $15–17M, lifetime AppMagic net IAP would be about **$165–185M by end-September 2026**. This is an unverified extrapolation; no Q3 2026 data point was found. Adding ads (30–40% share per DoF) would put gross lifetime revenue well above $200M.
- Pixel Flow skews heavily to iOS and Tier-1 markets. It had 13.2M Google Play installs in September 2026 against 26.4M total downloads by around May 2026, so iOS is probably at least half of its downloads.
- The competitive live ops (Pixel Arena, Fire Quest), Pixel Pass and No-Ads bundle were all live by Feb–Mar 2026, 6–7 months after launch. Their exact introduction dates are unknown (see Gaps).
- Team size is reported as ~10 (DoF, WIRED) and ~20 (Scopely, Kidscreen). The team probably doubled around the Scopely deal.

### Gaps
- No verified July–September 2026 monthly revenue or rank for Pixel Flow was found. The latest hard tracker points are H1 2026 ($104M) and ">$125M in under a year" (6 Aug 2026).
- No public changelog separates launch features from features added later, so the introduction of Pixel Arena, Fire Quest, Pixel Pass, Connected Pig and double blocks cannot be dated precisely.
- No second Loom Games title or announced spin-off was found. Scopely's "franchise" plan has not been detailed.
- No France- or EU-specific rank or revenue split for Pixel Flow was found. The DoF "US ≈ 9% of revenue" figure looks erroneous and could not be cross-checked.

## Q2: Pixel Flow clones and "conveyor puzzle" successors in 2025–2026: how many, who succeeded, who failed

### Takeaway
There are hundreds of clones by raw count: 100+ "on store" by March 2026, and 137 "pixel"-named plus 194 "flow"-named games launched in H1 2026 alone. Only about 15 of the top 5,000 publishers shipped a serious "similar game", and fewer than 10 fast-followers reached meaningful scale:
- Yarn Loop (Spyke/Combo, TR)
- Voodoo's Sand Loop, Marble Sort and Loop Sort
- Moon Active's Jelly Busters
- ABI's Colony Flow
- Bravestars' Block Loop Shooter
- GOODROID's Bus Traffic Fever, a combo game

The most literal clones, all from Amobear/AVN in Vietnam, scaled fastest but were removed from stores or sunset. In April 2026 Pixel Flow still held about 60% of the sort+conveyor category. In H1 2026 every follower was at least 2x smaller.

### Inferences
- The "conveyor/loop" sub-genre shows extreme winner-take-most dynamics. The leader holds about 60% of category revenue. Five to eight followers share most of the rest, each typically making $1–25M in H1 2026. Hundreds of long-tail titles have fewer than 100K installs.
- Every successful follower came from a publisher with its own UA machine and live-ops experience: Spyke, Voodoo, Moon Active, ABI and GOODROID. No indie or self-published small-team conveyor clone appears among the winners in the sources found.
- Literal clones make money fast, for example about $20M in weeks for Color Blaze Shooter. Once the original has a well-funded owner (Scopely), store takedowns and US lawsuits make that strategy short-lived.

### Gaps
- No complete count of Pixel Flow clones exists. The "137 pixel / 194 flow" figure is name-based, and "100+" / "at least 15 of the top 5,000 publishers" are analyst estimates.
- No specific Pixel Flow-like title was found from Rollic, Homa, SayGames, Supersonic, Azur, CrazyLabs, Century, Dream, Playtika, Peak, Kwalee or Fomo. Rollic's 2026 hits are other sort/block formats (see Q3).
- No outcome (ruling, settlement or injunction) was found for Loom v. Amobear or Funfinity v. ABI.

## Q3: Jam-type and adjacent sort/screw/block games: publishers, launches, revenue/downloads, trend

### Takeaway
The first-generation "dock sort" jam wave has matured: Bus Jam (Rollic, June 2023), Seat Away, Bus Out and Parking Jam. Its original titles are no longer among the top 20 hybrid-casual earners. The money moved to three places:
- Block-jam: Color Block Jam, with $148M lifetime IAP, now declining from its 2025 peak.
- Screw: Screwdom, with $120M lifetime IAP and holding steady.
- Sort/loop: Pixel Flow and its followers, which are rising.

The bus and jam theme is being revived through combos. GOODROID's Bus Traffic Fever reached about $400K/day in August 2026. A separate, mostly ad-funded download giant has emerged in arrows/escape and jigsaw-solitaire.

### Cited Findings

**Genre lineage (dates and mechanics)**
- The dock-sort timeline, per [Gamigion/M. Emery, 11 Mar 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/how-did-sort-puzzle-dominate-hybrid-casual/):
  - Water Sort launched in June 2020, and about 800 stack-sort clones followed in 2020–23.
  - Goods Sort (Shinrays) launched in November 2022 and took 8 months to reach $10M ARR.
  - Block Jam 3D (Voodoo, June 2022) added pathing and character sorting.
  - **Bus Jam (Rollic, June 2023)** "adds color containers ('bus'), defining the DSP format" and took 6 months to $10M ARR. Its key innovation is "capacity-constrained staging slots ('docks'), which create engineered losses and near-losses. Borrowed from Tile Match."
  - Hexa Sort (Lion) launched in June 2023.
  - In 2024–2026 studios added "conveyor belts, shooter entities" to the format, which is Pixel Flow's lineage.
- Block puzzles evolved in three generations, per [Gamigion/A. Demirel, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/block-puzzle-is-not-the-new-thing/):
  - Generation 1 is gamified Tetris (Block Blast) and is "basically exhausted".
  - Generation 2 is static designed boards (Color Block Jam) and is "stable".
  - Generation 3 is themes plus toolkits (car/bus jam, gecko/snake, arrows), where "the creative space is wide open".
- DoF on third-generation block leaders: Car Jam, Bus Escape, Crowd Express and Gecko Out show "declining downloads last year" but growing IAP. Block Blast reached "30 million installs per month" in early 2025 on ads only — [DoF, Jan 2026](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/1/19/from-tetris-to-block-blast-why-block-puzzles-never-stop-printing)

## Q6: What differentiated successful fast-followers from failed clones (design, meta, theme, art, live ops, UA, legal)

### Takeaway
The winners did not copy the core loop more faithfully. They did at least three of the following:
1. Added a twist visible in ads that is also physically "relatable": sand or marble physics (Voodoo), yarn theme (Spyke), ants (ABI).
2. Matched or beat Pixel Flow's retention (D30 of 12–17%).
3. Rebuilt the invisible structure: pressure/release level rhythm, competitive streak live ops aligned to difficulty, a tight economy, and controlled fail intensity.
4. Fed UA with hundreds of creatives on AppLovin and Mintegral.

Pure clones scaled fastest but died. They were removed after IP action (Color Blaze Shooter, about $20M), sunset (Color Cube Breaker), or starved when UA creatives ran out (Hexa Away). Legal risk sharply increased in 2026 (US suits, store takedowns, the "Flow" name).

### Cited Findings

**UA differentiation and retention (Emery's scorecard, April 2026)** — [Gamigion/M. Emery](https://www.gamigion.com/what-pixel-flow-like-games-are-worth-your-attention/)
- Yarn Loop: UA differentiation "Subtle", because the "pig shooters swapped with 'yarn spools'" and creatives "look quite similar to Pixel Flow". Retention "Superb – 17% D30".
- Sand Loop: UA differentiation "Great – the only Pixel Flow clone to differentiate meaningfully AND in a direction consistent with relatable, real-world physics". D30 9%.
- Marble Sort: "Good", relying on "faux 'marbles + gravity physics' to stand out as tangible and familiar". D30 12%.
- Color Cube Breaker: "None. Literal clone… But was able to launch and scale FAST". It was later sunset.
- Hotpot Fever: "Good" thematic execution but "relatability takes a hit". D30 5%.

**Sort retention benchmark**
- Queue-sort games aspiring to top-grossing compete against install-weighted D1/D7/D30 of **52/27/14** (US, Android). "Conveyor-ized games like Pixel Flow, Sand Loop, Marble Sort and Yarn Loop" pull the average D30 above 10% — [Gamigion/M. Emery, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/retention-benchmark-for-sort-puzzle-games-in-2026/)

**Hidden structure (why latecomers underperformed)**
- The analyst played four latecomers to level 100 and compared them with Pixel Flow — [Gamigion/S. Kim, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.gamigion.com/why-the-latecomers-could-not-replicate-pixel-flow/). Findings:
  - Each latecomer failed differently: "weak release", "drag" from over-dense pixel maps, a "collapsed back-half curve", or "weak amplitude/failure pressure".
  - Latecomer maps "looked like they would perform well as UA creatives" but had "unnecessarily high Pixel density".
  - The latecomers lacked early competitive live ops like Pixel Arena and Fire Quest.
  - Normal-level gaps were longer: 3.4–3.5 on average, with stretches of 8–10, versus Pixel Flow's 2.1.
  - Differential coin rewards did not change behavior because they were not tied to item prices.
  - Conclusion: "The latecomers copied the surface, but they did not replicate the structure that made that surface actually work."
- Three top sort games use three different "emotional contracts": Magic Sort sells comfort, Knit Out sells competence ("key offer fires after a win") and Pixel Flow sells pressure. Knit Out's revenue per download is "nearly double" that of bigger games — [Gamigion/O. Fomina](https://www.gamigion.com/pixel-flow-magic-sort-knit-out-why-players-can-not-stop/)
- DoF: "the real barrier isn't creativity. It's operation." Pixel Flow "didn't invent a new genre… it executed extremely well" — [DoF](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)

### Inferences
- For Mix Rush, the evidence gives a practical checklist:
  - **(a)** The color-mixing rule must be visible within 3 seconds of an ad. Emery's criterion is that theme swaps without visible mechanical change failed to separate followers in UA.
  - **(b)** Aim for D30 ≥ 12–17%, the level of the successful conveyor-ized followers.
  - **(c)** Ship competitive streak live ops and a tight economy tuned to the difficulty rhythm before scaling.
  - **(d)** Plan for creative volume: winners ran 600–1,100 creatives, and each pixel-art level can double as a new ad.
  - **(e)** Avoid Pixel Flow's protected expression (piggy shooters, belt around the picture, UI) and avoid "Flow"/"Pixel Flow"-like naming.
- The winners' base rates are sobering for an indie. Only 0.6% of Q1 2026 hybrid-casual launches reached $10M ARR. The successful Pixel Flow followers were all well-funded publishers, and there is no documented indie winner in this wave. A publisher deal or a very differentiated hook is the realistic path. This is an inference from the cited base rates.

### Gaps
- No quantified A/B evidence was found on the effect of specific twists, such as physics or theme, on CPI or LTV. The differentiation evidence is analyst judgment plus D30 and revenue outcomes.
- No EU/France-specific ruling on hybrid-casual puzzle clones since 2020 was found. The outcomes of the 2025–2026 US suits are unknown.
- No data was found on indie or self-published conveyor games that succeeded without a publisher. Pixel Flow itself was self-published but by a team with 120+ prior launches.
