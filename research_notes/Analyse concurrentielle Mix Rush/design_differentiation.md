# Design, player-psychology & marketing lessons from jam / sort / conveyor puzzles → improvements & differentiation for "Mix Rush" (color-mixing puzzle)

Notes compiled 2026-09-28. Time labels: [2026], [2025], [Background ≤2024]. Reliability flags used below: (primary) = official store page / publisher / help center; (analyst) = Deconstructor of Fun, AppMagic, PocketGamer.biz, Naavik, etc.; (fan site); (review aggregator); (search-snippet only) = the page could not be opened (bot wall / paywall / 403), so the claim comes from the search engine's excerpt of that page and should be treated as lower confidence.

Mix Rush recap (for the writer): pixel-art picture to paint; player takes only PRIMARY pots (R/Y/B, later white/black) from a jammed pile (only unblocked pots can be taken); drops each into one of 4–5 "mixing bowls" (buffer slots); a bowl whose color matches exposed pixels auto-paints them; B+Y in a bowl = green, etc.; wrong mixes clog bowls; lose when all bowls clogged; win when picture complete. Planned progression: primaries lv 1–10, secondaries 11–30, white→pastels 31–60, black/brown 61–100, obstacles 100+ (frozen pots, hidden pots, leaking bowl, timed boss levels). Monetization: boosters (extra bowl, undo, pipette, magic mixer), lives, rewarded ads; meta: gallery/museum of finished paintings.

## 1. Why the leading jam/sort/conveyor puzzles engage and monetize (core loop, readability, fail design, difficulty, level counts, sessions, FTUE, streaks, lives, boosters, continues, live ops, meta) — and what changed in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
The 2025–26 winning template is: an instantly readable, deterministic "clear the jam by color" board + a small buffer (tiles / slots / tray) whose saturation is the (only) lose condition + a long fail-free, ad-free onboarding (~20–30 min) + later hard/super-hard spikes monetized by boosters, continues and "fail offers" + light meta and daily/streak/race events. Analysts now single out the *timing of the first real difficulty peak* as the best predictor of revenue per download, and in a clone-saturated 2026 market they see "familiar templates with small but meaningful twists" winning — which is exactly Mix Rush's positioning (Pixel Flow-like template + mixing twist).

### Inferences
- **Mix Rush's bowls = the genre's proven buffer.** Bus Jam tiles, Pixel Flow's 5 slots and Block Jam 3D's dock all make "buffer full = lose" the single, readable failure; players already understand it. But Mix Rush adds a *second* failure path (wrong mix → clogged bowl). Two fail vectors raise cognitive load; the UI must make them one story ("a bowl is lost when it turns to sludge; you lose when no clean bowl remains") and each clog must be visibly the player's own choice (deterministic, like CBJ/Pixel Flow — "no randomness saving you").
- **Re-time the first difficulty peak and decouple it from the mixing tutorial.** Evidence: first peak timing predicts RPD; Pixel Flow's long fail-free onboarding beat Magic Sort's early spikes; CBJ was ad-free for 20–30 min and started hard levels/ads around level 14. The current plan (primaries 1–10, secondaries 11–30) risks stacking the first real challenge on the moment mixing is introduced (lv 11). Recommendation: show the first guided mix very early (lv 2–4, as the "wow"), keep levels ~1–15/20 fail-free and ad-free (~20–30 min), place the first "hard" level after that (≈ lv 15–25), then use a saw-tooth (hard / super-hard) cadence.
- **Novelty cadence.** Heavy players can clear ~60 levels on day 1 (Magic Sort), and CBJ "ramps up pretty fast" with new blockers. Keeping all obstacles (frozen/hidden pots, leaking bowl) until lv 100+ means engaged players hit a long stretch where the only novelty is a new color family. Interleave a light new twist every ~10–20 levels (e.g., a frozen pot at ~lv 20, hidden pots at ~lv 40), keeping color-family introductions separate from blocker introductions so only one new concept arrives at a time.
- **Content volume.** Given ~60 levels/day for engaged players and 2,000–6,000-level live games, plan a level-generation pipeline (pixel-art → level + solver that proves determinism/solvability and estimates difficulty). A few hundred levels at soft launch is a reasonable floor (inference; no public "levels at launch" benchmark was found).
- **Price anchors for Mix Rush** (from CBJ/Pixel Flow store pages): coin packs $1.99–$29.99, bundles $9.99–$34.99, No Ads $7.99–$11.99 (bundle it with some boosters), a $4.99 "fail offer" on the fail screen, a ~$9.99 pass; escalating in-level continue price (CBJ +1,000 coins per extra continue).
- **Fail screen = highest-intent moment.** Show the unfinished picture with the % painted / pixels left (honest near-miss framing) and offer the booster that fixes *this* failure ("+1 bowl", "rinse a clogged bowl", "undo last pot"). Keep it honest — the near-miss technique is gambling-derived and fake "so close" framing is a reputational risk.
- **Streaks and events only after habit forms.** Adopt a capped 3-step win-streak ladder (free pre-level boosters, e.g., pipette → extra bowl), but introduce it after the first difficulty peak, since PocketGamer.biz says early streak pressure worsened Magic Sort churn. For an indie: one daily event + one async race/leaderboard (Pixel Flow's Fire Quest/Pixel Arena model, which runs on off-the-shelf Nakama tournaments) is enough at launch.
- **Pick a "what we sell" identity.** A mixing puzzle naturally "sells competence" (knowing recipes, planning bowls), like Knit Out, whose RPD ($3.41) is 2× Magic Sort's. Frame boosters as tools of mastery (pipette, magic mixer) rather than "skip" buttons.

### Gaps
- No verified public data on a fixed hard/super-hard cadence (e.g., "every 5 or 10 levels") for these titles; the CBJ "hard from lv 14" claim comes from a fan site.
- No public "number of levels at launch" for Pixel Flow, CBJ, Bus Jam, Seat Away, Screw Jam or Magic Sort.
- Seat Away and Screw Jam (Rollic) specific deconstructions were not accessible; a "1000+ levels" Seat Away claim appeared only on store listings that may belong to clones, so it is not used.
- Lives regeneration time (commonly 30 min) is only supported by an unverifiable review quote (see §3).
- Coin prices of Pixel Flow boosters were not found.

## 2. Pixel Flow specifics (picture reveal, conveyor capacity, retention/monetization drivers)

### Takeaway
Pixel Flow (Loom Games, launched Aug 17 2025) combines (1) an instantly legible goal: a recognizable picture emerging pixel by pixel; (2) deterministic but execution-heavy play (speed, timing, "slinging" past the tray limit) that "sells intensity"; (3) the category's longest fail-free onboarding; (4) monetization aligned with gameplay (interstitials on fail/after wins, rewarded ads only for lives, no rewarded bypass of difficulty); (5) artwork targets that produce endless, non-fake ad creatives. Results: ~51 min/day over ~9.4 sessions, RPD $4.33, >$125M IAP in under a year, $1B+ valuation — and a wave of clones.

### Cited Findings
- Genre framing: a "Color Shooter / Sorting Shooter" with "shooters with a limited number of bullets" that "only clear blocks of its own color"; "a conveyor belt and a rotating tray", "a bench system where shooters come back after firing", and "strict slot limits, which define when you lose"; it "deliberately reintroduces speed and execution into a genre that has spent years removing them"; players can "chain actions to stack up to 10 units, temporarily push past the tray limit" if their timing is right; "fully deterministic", "no randomness saving you"; "double blocks" that "consume two slots" arrive mid-progression — (analyst) [Deconstructor of Fun, Feb 13 2026](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- Numbers, Dec 2025 (4 months after launch): "$300K+ per day from IAP", "30–40% of revenue from ads", "$550K total per day", "around 1M DAU", "200K downloads per day"; monetization: interstitials on failure and optional post-win, "limited rewarded ads for extra lives only", "No rewarded shortcuts that let players bypass difficulty", Remove Ads converts well because ad placement is "aggressive" after level 20; early retention "relatively modest" but the long-term curve stays strong; visuals: "superhero-adjacent characters", "cute animals, especially cats", a generic but proven style chosen to keep acquisition costs low; made by "a lean team of roughly 10 people" in "less than ~3 months" (Loom founded July 2025) — (analyst) [Deconstructor of Fun, Feb 13 2026](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- "colored shooters clear matching pixel blocks to reveal an image underneath"; constraints = slot limits, conveyor, rotating tray, recovery bench; "slinging" mechanic; "better mid-term retention among engaged players", "casual players drop off earlier due to difficulty"; "Interstitials run on failure and after wins"; "ads and IAP reinforce each other rather than compete"; no random reshuffles and no airplane-mode exploit — (analyst) [AppSamurai, Apr 8 2026](https://appsamurai.com/blog/pixel-flow-an-analysis-of-its-rapid-rise/). Caution: the same page reports the US at "about 9%" and the UK at "around 5%" of revenue. That conflicts with Pixel Flow's US top-20 grossing rank ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/scopely-acquires-majority-stake-in-istanbuls-pixel-flow-developer-loom-games/)), so it is probably a download share or an error.
- Design logic: the tray is "part of your active planning" rather than a hidden penalty; "artwork-shaped targets" (emojis, planets); the conveyor adds "dynamism"; "gameplay and monetization logic were aligned"; the team previously made Twisted Tangle (ad-heavy) and moved to IAP-heavy — (analyst) [Deconstructor of Fun, Mar 10 2026](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/pixel-flow-and-the-rise-of-sort-puzzles). Search excerpt of the same article: "watching a pixelated image come together as blocks disappear is immediately satisfying to watch, even for those who've never played"; the targets produce "huge creative variation without faking the product" → "volume, diversity, lower disappointment, and faster creative production"
- Engagement & monetization identity: "51.4 average minutes per day, 9.4 sessions per day, 1.9M DAU"; RPD $4.33 (#2 by growth); "sells intensity"; "pressure that feels controllable produces engagement"; longest fail-free onboarding — (analyst) [PocketGamer.biz, May 14 2026](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/one-genre-three-strategies-how-magic-sort-knit-out-and-pixel-flow-are-redefining-sort-puzzle-monetisation/)
- Live ops & tech: Fire Quest and Pixel Arena, daily 24-h competitive events with leaderboards; Nakama Tournaments API; cloud save; Google Play Games / Game Center sign-in; 10M+ players — (partner) [Heroic Labs](https://heroiclabs.com/blog/pixel-flow-highlight-announcement/)
- Store facts: one-tap, one-handed play; 5 waiting slots; conveyor capacity; 4.7★ / 161K ratings; IAP list (see §1) — (primary) [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pixel-flow/id6751056652)
- Clones: Vietnamese "Color Pixel Shooter" reached "$140K per day" with "67%" of revenue from the US — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream); "Color Blaze Shooter… $8 million over four months" — [AppMagic H1 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-casual-games-in-h1)
- Discrepancies to note: team ~10 at launch (DoF) vs ~20 at acquisition (PocketGamer.biz, AppSamurai), which suggests growth; DAU ~1M in Dec 2025 (DoF) vs 1.9M in May 2026 (PocketGamer.biz); release described as "late 2025" by some news vs Aug 17 2025 (DoF, AppMagic, Heroic Labs); Pixel Flow's rating is 4.7★ on the US App Store vs "4.3/5 from 178.1K reviews" in a review aggregator (§3), likely a different store or date.

### Inferences
- **Why the reveal motivates** (inference, backed by DoF's "immediately satisfying… even for those who've never played"): the picture is a visible progress bar (goal-gradient: effort rises near completion), an unfinished image creates closure tension, and the finished picture is a collectible trophy. Mix Rush inherits all three, and the museum meta extends the trophy value. Keep the reveal fast and "juicy": particles, sound, and a final zoom-out plus "add to museum".
- **Mapping to Mix Rush.** Pixel Flow pig ammo → paint quantity per pot or bowl (show the number on each pot/bowl); 5 slots → 4–5 bowls; conveyor capacity → optional "flow" of auto-painting. Pixel Flow shows that *deterministic + execution/timing* keeps sessions intense. Mix Rush's jammed-pile + bowl design is closer to Bus Jam/CBJ (planning, little time pressure). The name "Rush" promises speed: either add a tempo element (e.g., auto-paint flow along a conveyor, "sling" combos when several matching pots are dropped quickly) or be aware that the name over-promises.
- **Monetization blueprint to copy.** Rewarded ads only for lives/continues, no rewarded bypass of difficulty (protects IAP value); interstitials on fail and (later) after wins; a Remove Ads IAP whose value rises with ad pressure after ~lv 20; IAP-first with ads as 30–40% of revenue.
- **Clone economics.** Pure clones can make money fast ($140K/day; $8M in 4 months) but compete with a Scopely-backed original and 300+ "pixel/flow" launches in H1'26. Mix Rush needs its twist to be visible in the first 5 seconds of an ad and in the first minute of play.

### Gaps
- No published retention curve (D1/D7/D30) for Pixel Flow; only qualitative "modest early, strong long-term".
- No public list of Pixel Flow boosters with coin prices, nor exact lives/regeneration rules.
- No quantitative evidence isolating the "picture reveal" as a retention driver (the attribution is analyst opinion).

## 3. Player complaints and churn causes (store reviews, aggregators, analysts)

### Takeaway
Across Pixel Flow and Color Block Jam, the recurring 1-star themes are: interstitials after every level (even after declining a rewarded ad), strict timers seen as "artificial difficulty to farm money", levels seen as unsolvable without paying, life lock-outs, crashes or app-switches counted as a loss, and expensive power-ups. Ratings still sit at 4.6–4.7★, so players love the core loops and monetization pressure is the main driver of negative sentiment and churn. For a color game there is an extra risk: misread colors will be perceived as "unfair/random" deaths.

### Inferences (recommendations for Mix Rush)
- **Ads:** no interstitials in the first ~20–30 min (CBJ's ad-free window); never show an interstitial right after the player declines a rewarded ad; cap frequency (e.g., fail-only at first, post-win later); offer a No Ads bundle and honor it after reinstall (restore purchases).
- **Timers:** no timer in core levels. CBJ timers are its most cited complaint, and Mix Rush's appeal (planning mixes) conflicts with time pressure. Make "timed boss levels" opt-in, generous, or reward-only (a failure there should not cost a life).
- **Solvability promise:** solver-validate every level and say so in-game ("every level can be solved without boosters", as Rollic's help center does). Because the core is deterministic, show the player *why* they failed (e.g., highlight the pot that caused the sludge).
- **State persistence:** save mid-level state; an app switch or phone call must never cost a life. This is a cheap fix for a documented Pixel Flow 1-star cause.
- **Lives:** keep the standard 5 lives but give generous refills early (e.g., unlimited lives during the first session or as streak and event rewards). The "why give lives" complaint suggests hard lock-outs hurt casual players.
- **Booster pricing:** make at least one booster per day earnable in play; players notice when power-ups cost 1,900+ coins against small level rewards.
- **Color-specific churn (inference):** a misread shade (colorblindness, small screen, pastel vs primary) creates a death the player perceives as random or unfair, the same emotion as "unsolvable level" reviews. Accessibility is a retention feature here (see §4).

## 4. Color-mixing-specific risks: color vision deficiency, cognitive load of subtractive mixing, shade readability

### Takeaway
Mix Rush's palette (R, Y, B → orange, green, purple; plus white tints, black shades and brown) sits exactly on the most common color-vision-deficiency confusions: red/green, blue/purple, and reds/greens/oranges/browns. These affect ~8% of men and 0.5% of women, and the accessibility standard is that color alone must not carry essential information. Players' mental model is the school RYB wheel (R+Y=orange, Y+B=green, R+B=purple), which naive RGB blending contradicts (yellow+blue → gray), so mix results must be authored rather than computed. A ready-made symbol code built on RYB + white/black, whose symbols combine the way paints mix (ColorADD), exists and has a mainstream game precedent (UNO ColorADD, 2017), but it requires a commercial license.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence & typical confusions**
- "approximately 1 in 12 men (8%) and 1 in 200 women"; ~300 million people worldwide; red/green is the most common form; "someone with red/green colour blindness is likely to confuse blue and purple because they can't 'see' the red element of the colour purple"; "problems can arise across the entire colour spectrum potentially affecting perception of all reds, greens, oranges, browns, purples, pinks and greys" — (primary/NGO) [Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/). A search excerpt from the same site adds "Even black can be confused as dark red, dark green or dark blue/purple" (search-snippet).
- Tritan (blue-yellow) deficiency, which is rare: confusion between blue and green, purple and red, yellow and pink — (search-snippet summary of NEI/EnChroma results) [NEI: Types of CVD](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness/types-color-vision-deficiency)

**Accessibility standards & how color puzzles solve it**
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: "Ensure no essential information is conveyed by a fixed colour alone"; "around 8-10% of males" have red-green deficiency; "over half a million current Madden NFL users are colorblind" (EA Tiburon); recommended fix: "use colour as a back-up for another means of communicating the information, such as text or a symbol, pattern or shape"; presets for deuteranopia/protanopia/tritanopia and free color choice are acceptable. Palette filters are discouraged as the primary fix: they "change colors of elements that do not need to be changed, unnecessarily affect aesthetics, and introduce new clashes". Examples: Puzzle Retreat (symbol + color), Two Dots (colorblind mode), Hue (colorblind mode), Auralux (color customization) — (primary) [gameaccessibilityguidelines.com](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-colour-alone/)
- Puzzle Bobble 3D: Vacation Odyssey ships Red/Green, Blue/Yellow and "Universal" modes, where Universal replaces colors "with black, white or patterns" — (search-snippet) [Survios](https://survios.com/studio/color-blindness-accessibility-modes-revealed-for-puzzle-bobble-3d-vacation/); in puzzle games "matching mechanics should rely on both color and unique symbols or patterns" — (search-snippet) [AbleGamers](https://ablegamers.org/unlockingaccessibilitypuzzlegames/)
- WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.11: graphical objects "required to understand the content" need "a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s)"; a "change of hue alone to convey the value or state… is likely to fail the Use of color criterion" — (primary) [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
- ColorADD (Miguel Neiva, Portugal): five base symbols (two triangles, a diagonal line, a solid square, an empty square) for red, blue, yellow, black and white. Secondary colors combine the base symbols (e.g., orange = red + yellow symbols), and light/dark tones are built by combination. Used in transport, schools (Viarco pencils), healthcare and paint cans. Mattel "launched a colorblind-friendly version of Uno that utilizes ColorADD" (Sept 2017). Won the 2013 Vodafone Foundation Mobile For Good accessibility award. It is proprietary: "Usage must be licensed" via Miguel Neiva & Associados (fees undisclosed); only schools and universities get pro-bono licenses — [Wikipedia: ColorADD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ColorADD). The official site describes the logic as "Color Addition Theory (e.g. Blue+Yellow=Green)" with derived colors "orange, green, purple and brown" and "dark or white tones" — (primary, search-snippet; the site returned 403) [ColorADD code](https://www.coloradd.net/en/coloradd-code/)

**Mental model of mixing (RYB vs RGB/CMY)**
- RYB predicts red+yellow = orange, blue+yellow = green, red+blue = purple. It is "scientifically obsolete" and "can be considered an approximation of the CMY color model", yet it remains embedded in art education (Bauhaus, Parsons), so it is the public's default model — [Wikipedia: RYB color model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RYB_color_model)
- With school-grade paints, RYB mixing gives dull or muddy purples and greens; "red plus blue only makes a clean purple when both the red and the blue you pick already lean toward purple" — (search-snippet) [PaintKit Studio](https://paintkitstudio.com/blogs/blog/color-mixing-for-beginners)
- Digital RGB blending contradicts paint expectations: "mixing a yellow RGB color with a blue RGB color results in gray instead of green" — (search-snippet) [USPTO patent 10269142](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10269142); students tend to assume the primaries of light are red, blue and yellow, a hard-to-overcome preconception — (search-snippet) [arXiv 1009.4458](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.4458)
- Technical fix for "paint-like" digital mixing: Mixbox treats RGB colors as pigments using the Kubelka–Munk model ("RGB in, RGB out"), so "blue and yellow mix to green, the same way real pigments do", with "saturated gradients" and natural secondaries; it ships in Rebelle 5 Pro and the Flip Fluids Blender add-on — [Mixbox / Secret Weapons](https://scrtwpns.com/mixbox/); [Mixbox docs](https://scrtwpns.com/mixbox/docs/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mixbox-realistic-pigment-based-color-mixing-library-from-ebsynth-creators)

**Existing color-mixing games (how others handle the cognitive load)**
- Hex Mixer: Color Mixing Puzzle: recreate target colors "as precisely as possible by adding drops one at a time", mixing "according to RGB values", with levels that introduce concepts gradually — (primary, search-snippet) [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hex-mixer-color-mixing-puzzle/id6756682061); Mix Colors!: 500+ levels, reviewers praise its calm and its mixing physics — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mix-colors/id1476681691); Color Merge Puzzle: initial levels need ~60 shades made from base colors — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/color-merge-puzzle/id6449178197); Trycolors: "physically accurate paint mixing" guess-the-mix game — [Trycolors](https://trycolors.com/games/guess-mix) (all search-snippet level)

### Inferences (risk matrix & recommendations)
- **Confusable pairs in the planned palette** (inference from the cited CVD data): red (primary) vs green (secondary) is the #1 red/green-CVD confusion; blue (primary) vs purple (secondary) is the classic confusion; yellow/orange/green/brown confuse each other; pastels (pink vs lilac vs peach, mint vs lemon) are close in lightness; shades (dark red vs brown vs dark green vs black) are hard for everyone on small screens. Almost every level after lv 11 would contain at least one confusable pair, so **symbols must be ON by default, not hidden in a settings menu**.
- **Compositional glyphs as the core UI** (strong recommendation): give each primary a shape; a secondary shows both parent shapes; a white tint adds a "light" mark and a black shade a "dark" mark; brown = all three primaries. This is the ColorADD logic (license it, or design an original glyph system on the same principle after legal advice). It does double duty: it makes the game colorblind-playable, and **the glyph *is* the recipe**, which removes most of the RYB/RGB cognitive-load risk for everyone. It is also a store-page and PR hook ("playable by colorblind players").
- **Author the mixing table; don't compute it.** Use a fixed RYB lookup (R+Y=O, Y+B=G, R+B=P, +W=tint, +K=shade, R+Y+B=brown) with vivid, saturated secondaries (players expect a nice purple, not muddy real-paint purple). Use Kubelka–Munk (Mixbox or equivalent; check the commercial license) only for the *animation* of the swirl, so the transition looks like paint, never gray.
- **Preview before commit.** When a pot is dragged or hovered over a bowl, show a ghost of the resulting color + glyph ("→ green ✓ matches 12 pixels" / "→ sludge ✗"). This keeps the genre's fast readability (CBJ/Pixel Flow are readable at a glance) despite the added mental step, and turns errors into choices rather than surprises.
- **Separate "sludge" from brown.** Real paint turns brown/gray when complements mix, but brown becomes a target color at lv 61–100. Make the clog state visually and semantically unmistakable (e.g., gray, bubbling, a ✗ glyph, a "blop" sound) and give brown its own glyph and recipe.
- **Lightness separation.** Within a level, choose target colors that differ in luminance, not only hue (WCAG's 3:1 contrast for graphical objects is a useful floor). Cap the number of simultaneous colors per level (e.g., ≤5–6), and never pair two pastels or two dark shades that differ only in hue until the player has glyph fluency.
- **Keep ratios out of the core loop.** Continuous or ratio mixing (Hex Mixer, Trycolors) produces a calm precision game, the opposite of the jam genre's instant readability. If ratios appear later ("2 yellow + 1 blue = lime"), make them a special mode (daily or "master" levels) with explicit UI.
- **Tertiaries** (red-orange, yellow-green, …) are hard to name and hard to tell apart on phones. Avoid them in the main path or treat them as rare, explicitly taught "master colors".
- **QA protocol:** run every level's palette through deuteranopia/protanopia/tritanopia simulation and small-screen/low-brightness checks, and include colorblind playtesters in soft launch.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures what mobile players *expect* from specific digital mixes (e.g., the % who expect R+B=purple vs magenta); the RYB-default claim rests on education sources.
- ColorADD license fees and whether its symbols may be adapted in a game are undisclosed; legal advice is needed.
- The Mixbox commercial license terms were not verified in this session.
- No data was found on the gender split of jam/sort audiences, which determines the actual share of CVD players (8% of men vs 0.5% of women).

## 6. Differentiation used by fast-followers & best candidates for Mix Rush (with consolidated recommendations)

### Takeaway
Fast-followers make money by copying the template and changing theme or presentation (Pixel Flow clones: $140K/day; $8M in 4 months; Magic Sort won with premium art + pure IAP; Knit Out with a yarn theme and "competence" positioning), but 2026 is saturated (137 "pixel" + 194 "flow" launches in H1'26), and analysts say "familiar templates with small but meaningful twists" win. ### Inferences — consolidated, prioritized recommendations for Mix Rush (each tied to the evidence above)

**A. Must-do (core-loop risk reducers)**
1. **Mixing in the first session.** First guided mix at lv 2–4 as the "wow"; mixing is the USP and the ad promise (§5 alignment). Keep ~lv 1–15/20 fail-free and ad-free (~20–30 min), with the first real peak after the habit forms (§1: first-peak timing → RPD; Pixel Flow vs Magic Sort).
2. **Readable mixing.** Authored RYB recipe table + drag preview ("→ green ✓ / → sludge ✗") + compositional glyphs ON by default (ColorADD-style; license or original). This solves colorblind accessibility (8% of men; red/green and blue/purple confusions hit R/G/B/P directly) and teaches recipes (§4).
3. **Fair clogs.** Sludge is visually distinct from brown and always the result of a visible choice. Add a recoverable path (a "rinse" booster or a slow-draining sink bowl) so one mis-tap does not doom a level with no counterplay; show why the fail happened (§3 "unsolvable/unfair" complaints; DoF: the tray as "active planning", not a hidden penalty).
4. **Deterministic, solver-validated levels** with an "every level solvable without boosters" promise (Rollic), plus mid-level state saving so app switches never cost a life (Pixel Flow 1-star cause).
5. **Palette discipline.** ≤5–6 colors per level, luminance-separated targets (WCAG 3:1 as a floor), pastels (white) and shades (black) introduced with glyph modifiers, CVD-simulation QA on every level.
6. **Monetization aligned with gameplay** (Pixel Flow): rewarded ads for lives and continues only; interstitials on fail first, post-win later; no interstitial right after a declined rewarded ad; No Ads $7.99–11.99 bundled with boosters; $4.99 fail offer; coin packs $1.99–29.99; ~$9.99 pass later. Booster map: extra bowl ↔ Bus Jam "extra tiles"; undo; pipette (make one exact pot); magic mixer (turn one sludge bowl into the needed color).
7. **No timers in core levels**; timed boss levels opt-in or reward-only (CBJ's #1 complaint).

**B. Should-do (differentiators with evidence of fit)**
8. **Famous public-domain paintings as "Masterpiece" levels** (Van Gogh, Monet, Hokusai, Vermeer…, as pixel-art reinterpretations) feeding the **museum meta**: wings by art movement, album completion rewards, shareable finished paintings. Rationale: instantly legible targets are Pixel Flow's UA engine; Happy Color proves the mass appeal of artwork completion; collectibles and renovation are proven sort-puzzle metas. 9. **"Mix of the Day" daily puzzle.** One shareable, Colorfle-like recipe puzzle per day with a streak that is "completable on a bad day". This builds a habit and organic sharing without heavy live-ops tech.
10. **Events after the habit forms.** A capped 3-win streak ladder, then one async race/leaderboard (Fire Quest/Pixel Arena analog on Nakama or a similar backend). Avoid early streak pressure (Magic Sort lesson).
11. **Sell competence.** Recipe book, "master colors", and mastery badges per color family; frame boosters as tools (Knit Out's positioning, RPD $3.41 ≈ 2× Magic Sort's).
12. **Tempo decision vs. the "Rush" name.** Either add a satisfying flow element (auto-paint streams, quick-drop combos like Pixel Flow's "slinging") or reconsider the name, since Pixel Flow's intensity is its engagement driver and CBJ-style planning without tempo does not deliver "rush".
13. **Interleave obstacles earlier.** A light new twist every ~10–20 levels instead of all blockers at lv 100+ (engaged players can do ~60 levels/day; CBJ ramps fast). Keep one new concept at a time.

**C. Could-do / later (weak or no direct evidence)**

15. **Real-time PvP race:** costly; start with async leaderboards (the Pixel Flow precedent is async/daily).
16. **Ratio mixing** ("2Y+1B = lime") only as a daily/master mode; continuous mixing belongs to calm niche games (Hex Mixer, Mix Colors!).
