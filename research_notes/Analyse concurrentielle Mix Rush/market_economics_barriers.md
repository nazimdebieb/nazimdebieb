## 1. Market size & growth: hybrid-casual and hybrid-casual puzzle, 2025 vs 2026

### Takeaway
Hybrid-casual (HC) is the only mobile-games segment still growing IAP meaningfully (about +20% in 2025). HC puzzle (sort / screw / block-jam / conveyor "Pixel Flow-like") is its hottest pocket: the top 10 HC puzzles made more than $270M IAP in Jan–May 2026, against about $340M in all of 2025, so the monthly run rate roughly doubled. Revenue is extremely concentrated, though (3 titles ≈ 42% of HC-puzzle revenue in H1 2026), and new games almost never reach the top charts (new releases = 2% of the top 300 in H1 2026, down from 8% in H1 2023).

### Inferences
- **Run rate:** the top 10 HC puzzles were at about $54M/month in Jan–May 2026 ($270M / 5), against about $28M/month in 2025 ($340M / 12), so roughly ×1.9. Caution: the page-summarising tool misread this as a decline. It is strong growth. It is IAP-only, and ads plausibly add another 30–45% of revenue on top (see Q4).
- **Concentration:** the top 3 (Pixel Flow + Screwdom + Color Block Jam) ≈ 42% of HC-puzzle revenue in H1 2026. The market is winner-take-most, and the long tail is thin.
- **Pixel Flow ARPDAU:** its implied ARPDAU was about $0.55 in Dec 2025 ($550K/day ÷ ~1M DAU), at the top of the HC range.
- **Who captures the growth:** incumbents (Rollic/Zynga/Take-Two, Scopely-backed Loom, experienced Turkish and Vietnamese studios). The segment is "penetrable" only in the sense that a small team built the #1 title in under 3 months, but that team was a veteran hit-maker.
- **Hypercasual:** it is not collapsing in downloads or time spent (Sensor Tower, Adjust), but the money has moved to hybrid monetisation (IAP + ads).

## 2. UA costs: CPI and CPM by platform and geo, privacy changes, cost of a CPI test

### Takeaway
UA got more expensive in 2025–26: global gaming CPI rose 30% to $0.56, with Europe reportedly up 47%. Casual/puzzle CPI on US Android is typically $1.50–3.50, Western Europe Android $0.60–1.50, and iOS runs 3–4x Android. A minimal, statistically usable CPI test (~300 installs) costs about $600–1,200 on Tier-1 Android. But CPI at scale runs 2–2.5x the test figure, and casual D30 ROAS is only about 15% on Android (≈47% on iOS), so paid growth needs months of working capital that a solo developer does not have.

### Cited Findings

- Regional CPI changes, same Adjust report [SN, not in the PPC Land article]: [GameDev Reports, Adjust 2026 summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report)
  - Europe +47% YoY ($0.36 → $0.53).
  - North America +31% ($1.28 → $1.68).
  - LATAM +40% (→ $0.14).
- Game Growth Advisor (17 Mar 2026), casual/puzzle **Android** CPI by geo [S]: [GGA CPI Benchmarks 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/)

  - Spent $250M on UA in 2023.
  - Produces 50,000 ad videos/year.
  - Serves 150B ad impressions/year.
- [BG] A Voodoo puzzle game succeeded despite a $1.60 CPI because it had "22% D7 and 30-minute average playtime" (Voodoo guide, Nov 2023) [P]: [Voodoo, "A Snackable Guide to Partnering with Voodoo"](https://voodoo.io/news/a-snackable-guide-to-partnering-with-voodoo)

### Inferences
- **Where to test vs where the money is:** for a France-based solo developer, France / Western Europe Android is the cheapest near-Tier-1 testbed ($0.60–1.50 CPI). The money in HC puzzles is US-heavy, though: the US is 58% of iOS ad revenue, and one Pixel Flow clone earned 67% of its revenue in the US (Q6). Tests therefore need a US read at some point, at $1.50–3.50 on Android and ×3–4 on iOS.
- **Test budget** (my estimate from GGA's ranges; not a quoted figure):
  - One CPI concept test (~300 installs) ≈ €550–1,100 on US/Tier-1 Android.
  - A first retention/monetisation read (1,000–3,000 installs) ≈ €1,000–5,000 on WE/US Android.
  - 3–5 iterations ≈ €5–20K.
- **Scaling cost:** scaling is out of reach for a solo budget. Pixel Flow ran about 200K downloads/day. Even 10K installs/day at a $2 CPI = $20K/day, with ~15% of it recovered by D30 on Android (Liftoff).
- **Measurement:** with Privacy Sandbox retired, Android keeps the advertising ID. Android stays the most measurable and cheapest platform for prototypes, while iOS measurement remains aggregated (ATT, AdAttributionKit).
- **Creative capacity:** it is a structural barrier. Incumbents ship dozens of creatives per week, and only ~5% of creatives win.

## 3. KPI benchmarks publishers require to scale a puzzle prototype

### Takeaway
None of the named publishers publishes a hard 2026 threshold sheet; all describe "flexible" or holistic evaluation. Triangulating from GameAnalytics and publisher disclosures:
- **To be top-10% on mobile**, a prototype needs about 40% D1, 11–12% D7 and about 40 min/day of playtime.
- **HC puzzles that scale** show about 20% D7 and about 10% D30 (Pixel Flow players spend about 1 h/day across about 10 sessions), with ARPDAU of $0.15–0.50.
- **Publishers trade off CPI against LTV**: Voodoo scaled a puzzle game at a $1.60 CPI because of 22% D7 and 30 min/day of playtime.
- **The funnel is brutal**: Voodoo launched about 0.4% of prototypes.

  | Metric | Median | Top 25% | Top 10% | Top 1% |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | D1 | ~20% | 30%+ | 40% | 64–68% |
  | D7 | <4% | 6–7% | 11–12% | 25%+ |
  | D30 | 0.69–0.79% | 1.6–1.8% | n/a | 13–15% |
  | Daily playtime | n/a | 22–24 min | 40–42 min | 94+ min |
  | Session length | n/a | 5.2 min | 8 min | 22 min |
  | Sessions/day | n/a | 5.3–5.7 | 9.6 | 14 |

  - D1 retention in 2025 declined compared with 2024.
- GGA (Mar–Apr 2026) [S]: [GGA CPI 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/); [GGA HC design 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)

- [BG] HC arcade-idle (Sensor Tower / Homa research) [SN]: [Udonis, Arcade Idle](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/arcade-idle)

- ROAS reference: casual D30 ROAS 47% on iOS and 15% on Android (Liftoff) [P]: [Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/blog/what-is-a-good-roas/)

### Inferences
My synthesis of a "green-light zone" for a 2026 HC-puzzle prototype (an estimate, not a publisher-stated threshold):
- **Marketability:** US Android test CPI ≲ $1–1.5, knowing it will rise ×2–2.5 at scale.
- **D1:** ≥ 35–40%.
- **D7:** ≥ 12–15% at prototype, ≥ 18–20% before scaling.
- **D30:** ≥ 6–10% in soft launch.
- **Playtime:** ≥ 30–40 min/day across 6–10 sessions.
- **ARPDAU:** ≥ $0.20, with a ROAS curve that credibly reaches 100% by D90–D180.

Two further points:
- Publishers now reason in **LTV/ROAS terms**, not fixed CPI cut-offs, so a higher CPI is acceptable when playtime and D7 are exceptional (Voodoo's $1.60-CPI puzzle).
- **Base rate per prototype:** Voodoo's 0.4% launch rate, AppMagic's 0.8% of new match-3 reaching $100K/month, and Azur's 2% of the top 300 being new all point to odds of about 1% or less.

### Gaps
- No 2026 KPI sheets published by Homa, SayGames, CrazyLabs, Kwalee, Rollic/Zynga, Azur, Lion Studios, Ruby Games or Tapnation were found. Their portals and blogs show processes, not numbers.
- A snippet gave puzzle-specific GameAnalytics retention (D1 31.85%, D7 12.18%, D30 5.35%), but I could not tie it to the GA report itself, so it is unverified.
- No public data on the share of prototypes that pass a CPI test (as opposed to Voodoo's launch rate).
- Kwalee's market-test guide on Medium returned 403, so it was not read.

## 4. Monetization model of jam/sort puzzles

### Takeaway
The leaders are genuinely hybrid, at roughly 55–70% IAP and 30–45% ads: Pixel Flow is 60–70% IAP; Sensor Tower's HC "lifestyle & puzzle" cluster is 59/41; a 1:1 Pixel Flow clone earned 50/50. The design pattern is:
- lives and timers,
- coin-bought boosters,
- **fail offers** at the moment of frustration,
- rewarded video to refill lives or earn coins,
- no-ads and pass offers.

HC ARPDAU is $0.15–0.50 (Pixel Flow ≈ $0.55), against $0.03–0.08 for ad-only hypercasual.

### Cited Findings
- Pixel Flow: IAP 60–70% and ads 30–40% of revenue [P]: [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- HC IAP/ads split by cluster (Sensor Tower *State of Gaming 2026*, via GGA): lifestyle & puzzle 59% IAP / 41% ads; sports & racing 71/29; action & strategy 81.9/18.1. Lancaric (2025): HC averages a "40–50% revenue profile driven by IAPs" [S]: [GGA HC design 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)
- The 1:1 Pixel Flow clone Color Blaze Shooter earned $10M from IAP + $10M from ads in about 4 weeks [P]: [AppBird](https://appbird.ai/blog/pixel-flow-copycat-20-million-gone/)
- Block Blast (generation 1) is ads-only with no IAP.   - Seasonal passes $8–20.
  - No-ads subscription $5–10/month.

### Inferences
- With ads only, a solo developer's game would earn hypercasual-like ARPDAU ($0.03–0.08), which cannot cover $0.60–3.50 CPIs. An **IAP layer** is therefore mandatory for paid UA: lives, boosters, fail offers, no-ads and a pass.
- IAP depends on **difficulty design**: hard "wall" levels where boosters feel like natural solutions. That means large volumes of designed levels (Q8).
- Pixel Flow's implied ARPDAU (≈ $0.55) shows what a top title reaches. A new entrant should budget for the low end ($0.10–0.20) in early soft launch (my estimate).

## 5. Publisher route vs self-publishing, and alternative platforms

### Takeaway
- **Publishers:** for a solo developer on mobile, the only realistic route to scale is a publisher, which funds CPI and retention tests and UA and recoups before sharing revenue. Odds per prototype are about 1% or less, and even hits pay studios about $1M+ on average (Voodoo).
- **Self-publishing on mobile** works for capitalised veteran teams (Loom / Pixel Flow), not for a limited-budget solo developer.
- **Web portals (Poki, CrazyGames)** are a cheap validation and revenue side-channel. The developer keeps a 50–70% share, and a good casual game earns about $200–2,000/month. **YouTube Playables** revenue sharing is still a pilot. **Apple Arcade** forbids IAP and ads, and its payouts have reportedly been declining.
- **France:** the CNC tax credit (up to 30%) exists, but it targets companies subject to corporate tax.

## 6. Fast-follow / clone risk and legal protection

### Takeaway
New HC-puzzle mechanics get cloned within weeks:
- Pixel Flow copycats started appearing when Loom was about 1 month old, with 2–4 clones per week.
- A 1:1 clone made $20M in about 4 weeks before both stores pulled it.
- A "structural" clone ran at about $140K/day.
- Color Block Jam spawned several generations of reskins.

Game mechanics are not copyrightable. Only code, art and assets (and arguably overall "look and feel"), plus trademarks, are protectable. In practice, takedowns need proof of asset or code theft and the resources to pursue it: Pixel Flow's high-profile removal came after Scopely backed Loom.

### Cited Findings
- Clone flow [SN]: [AppBird](https://appbird.ai/blog/pixel-flow-copycat-20-million-gone/); [Gamigion, Color Blaze Shooter removed](https://www.gamigion.com/pixelflow-copycat-color-blaze-shooter-removed-from-stores/)
  - "Loom Games was one month old when the copycats started appearing."
  - "Every week, 2–4 Pixel Flow clones drop, though most fail."
  - A 1:1 copy (Color Blaze Shooter) appeared about one month after Pixel Flow became a mega-hit.
- Color Blaze Shooter (Amobear, operating as AVN Globalis), per AppBird (12 Mar 2026) [P]: [AppBird](https://appbird.ai/blog/pixel-flow-copycat-20-million-gone/)
  - "Identical mechanics, matching visual aesthetic, the same core gameplay loop" (grid of color blocks, conveyor with shooters).
  - $20M in about 4 weeks ($10M IAP + $10M ads).
  - Removed from Google Play and the App Store on 1 Mar 2026.
  - "Removal typically requires proof of code or asset theft", and Scopely's Feb 2026 acquisition "provided the legal infrastructure".
  - "Hundreds of Pixel Flow-inspired games" were still on both stores.
- Color Pixel Shooter, a close structural clone by a Vietnamese studio, reached about $140K/day with 67% of revenue from the US [P]: [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- Color Block Jam "spawned a huge wave of copycats and reskins". Survivors include Shape Escape, Water Out and Wood Away; second-generation followers keep appearing, and a third generation of block puzzles has started [SN]: [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/color-block-jam-by-rollic-scaled-to-300k-a-day/)
- DoF (Jan 2026) [P]: [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/1/19/from-tetris-to-block-blast-why-block-puzzles-never-stop-printing)
  - "The moment a formula becomes legible, teams start racing to differentiate."
  - Third-generation themes already crowded: car/bus jams, snake/gecko, arrows.
  - There is "no real limit on how you can theme" the Color Block Jam formula.
- Rollic itself iterated a formula across Twisted Tangle, Seat Away and Screw Jam before Color Block Jam, i.e. publishers systematically re-theme proven cores [SN]: [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/color-block-jam-by-rollic-scaled-to-300k-a-day/)
- Legal [SN]: [FKKS](https://fkks.com/news/how-courts-view-copyright-protection-for-video-games); [Game Design Skills](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-development/mechanics-patents-copyrights/); [Argo Law, cloning & Tetris](https://argolawyer.com/legality-of-cloning-games/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/texas-court-affirms-game-mechanics-not-protected-under-copyright-law)
  - The US Copyright Office says copyright "does not protect the idea for a game, its name or title, or the method or methods for playing it".
  - Code and art are protected, and courts have considered overall look and feel (Tetris v. Xio is cited as the cloning precedent).
  - A 2025 Texas ruling (DaVinci v. Ziko, a board game) reaffirmed that mechanics are unprotected.
- AppBird's protective advice: "register trademarks early", document design records and development history, respond quickly to disputes, and build depth beyond core mechanics (content, live ops, community) [P]: [AppBird](https://appbird.ai/blog/pixel-flow-copycat-20-million-gone/)

### Inferences
- A **color-mixing twist is a mechanic**, so it is legally unprotectable. Studios with 50,000-video/year creative machines and existing HC-puzzle level pipelines (Voodoo, Rollic, Vietnamese/Turkish fast-followers) can ship a re-themed version within 2–6 weeks of it showing traction. The window of exclusivity is **weeks, not months**.
- **Defensibility levers for a solo developer:**
  - Partner with a publisher before or at the first signs of traction, so scale arrives before the clones.
  - Build level volume and quality, which are hard to copy at speed.
  - Use distinctive art/brand plus a registered trademark ("Mix Rush" at INPI/EUIPO) to enable store takedowns of confusingly similar names or icons.
  - Keep dated design records.
- The Mix Rush concept sits squarely in the most-cloned lane (conveyor/color like Pixel Flow, jam/sort like Color Block Jam), which is the third generation already crowding the space (DoF).

### Gaps
- No dated evidence found on the Bus Jam clone timeline (bus/car jam is named as crowded, but no dates or figures).
- French/EU law specifics (droit d'auteur on software and graphics, "parasitisme" claims) were not researched.
- INPI/EUIPO trademark fees were not verified.
- Availability of the name "Mix Rush" in the stores and trademark registers was not checked.

## 7. Organic and virality routes in 2026

### Takeaway
The sources contain essentially no verified case of a solo developer's hybrid-casual *mobile* puzzle breaking out organically with a low budget in 2024–2026. The documented breakouts (Pixel Flow, Color Block Jam, Screwdom, the clones) were driven by paid UA at scale by experienced teams or publishers. Organic levers do exist, but they mainly lower CPI and de-risk creative testing rather than replace paid UA:
- TikTok/Shorts-native UGC-style gameplay videos,
- web-portal discovery,
- YouTube Playables.

Organic, self-published breakouts by solo developers in 2025 happened on **PC/Steam** (streamer-driven), not in HC mobile puzzles.

### Cited Findings
- Short-form video is a competitor for attention: a "growing share of the audience chooses short-form video over a gaming session" [P]: [Azur](https://azurgames.com/blog/hypercasual-and-hybrid-casual-in-2026-full-report/)
- The web as a discovery funnel [P]: [Poki State of Web Gaming 2026](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)
  - 62% of web gamers downloaded or purchased games after discovering them on the web (72% of frequent players).
  - 46% of developers cite discoverability as a leading benefit.
  - Poki has 100M+ MAU.
- Tapnation (paid, but social-native): a CPI "as low as $0.20 on TikTok" for Tippy Toe [P]: [Tapnation](https://www.tap-nation.io/blog/kpis-that-matter-metrics-to-track-in-hybrid-casual-games/)
- Creative-driven discovery economics [S]: [GGA](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)
  - About 5% creative winner rate (Motion 2026).
  - Top accounts ship about 53 creatives/week (Liftoff 2026).
  - Playables have an 8–16x impression-to-install advantage (Liftoff 2025).
- Pixel Flow reached 10M+ players [SN]: [Heroic Labs](https://heroiclabs.com/blog/pixel-flow-highlight-announcement/). It had about 200K downloads/day by Dec 2025 while spending on UA from a veteran team [P]: [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- Block Blast! is cited with a brand-search score of 8.6: organic demand exists, but it was built after huge scale [P]: [GameDev Reports, Sensor Tower](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)
- Solo/indie organic successes on PC:
  - *Agreeee*: 30K+ Steam copies in about a month, via streamers and VTubers [SN]: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreeee)
  - *Strange Jigsaws*: self-published, IGF 2026 honourable mention [SN]: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Jigsaws)

### Inferences
- **Mix Rush's hook is inherently "satisfying" to watch** (colors blending, conveyor flow). That suits TikTok/Shorts gameplay clips and UGC-style ads, which are the cheapest way to find a winning creative angle before paying for CPI tests. Organic views should be treated as creative R&D and a CPI reducer, not as a growth engine.
- **Web portals plus Playables** are the only realistic "organic distribution" channels with built-in audiences for a solo developer, and they also provide gameplay data.
- **ASO/featuring** alone is unlikely to move an HC puzzle in a market where new releases are only 2% of the top 300 (Azur).

### Gaps

- No data on the impact of App Store/Google Play featuring or ASO for HC puzzles.
- Three snippets were unverified because their attribution is uncertain:
  - a TikTok case study "Homa – Clean It!: 2.5M installs in six months, −55% CPI, +60% CVR";
  - "gameplay UGC hybrids outperformed studio ads 3x on hook-rate" (Admiral);
  - "TikTok 15–25% lower CPI than Meta".

## 8. Development cost and time for a jam/sort puzzle MVP

### Takeaway
The core loop is cheap and fast to build:
- Pixel Flow was built in under 3 months by about 10 experienced people.
- Industry guides suggest HC MVPs of a few weeks to a few months (the often-quoted "6–10 weeks" is unverified; see Gaps).
- A CPI-test prototype needs only a few minutes of gameplay.
- A Unity build ports to the web in hours.

The real barrier is **content and operations**: large volumes of hand-tuned levels (Azur: block puzzle needs a "huge amount of content" plus level-design, balance and pacing expertise), a meta layer (4–6 extra weeks), live ops, and a continuous flow of ad creatives.

### Cited Findings
- Pixel Flow: development time under about 3 months, team of about 10, launched about 6 weeks after the studio was founded (Jul → 17 Aug 2025) [P]: [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/13/pixel-flow-the-publishers-dream)
- GGA (Apr 2026) [S]: [GGA HC design 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)
  - Minimum meta build: 4–6 weeks after a playable hook.
  - Soft launch needs 3–4 weeks of stable retention before scaling paid UA.
  - A hypercasual-to-hybrid migration takes 9–18 months.
  - 3–6 min sessions.
- Azur (Aug 2026) [P]: [Azur](https://azurgames.com/blog/hypercasual-and-hybrid-casual-in-2026-full-report/)
  - Block puzzle requires a "huge amount of content", skilled teams, and expertise in level design, balance and pacing, so "competing with them as a newcomer is exceptionally difficult".
  - Success requires a long-term approach, LiveOps, and multi-year content-production workflows.
- Poki (Jun 2026): the initial Unity → web port takes "1-to-2 hours"; optimisation is the main work [P]: [Poki](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)
- Level editors are a standard part of the casual-puzzle pipeline: designers build and test levels internally against balance sheets, and soft-launch data on level pass rates drives re-tuning [SN]: [CrazyLabs, level design in hyper-casual](https://www.crazylabs.com/blog/the-must-have-of-level-design-in-hyper-casual-mobile-games/); [Room 8 Studio, tile-puzzle level design](https://room8studio.com/news/smart-casual-the-state-of-tile-puzzle-games-level-design-part-1/)
- [BG] Voodoo's creative scale is 50,000 videos/year, which shows the parallel "creative production" workload behind UA [P]: [Voodoo](https://voodoo.io/news/voodoo-s-secret-sauce-from-0-to-250m-hybridcasual-revenue-in-3-years)

### Inferences
- **Solo MVP estimate** (my estimate, not sourced): 1.5–3 months for a CPI/retention-test build with 20–40 levels, basic lives and boosters, and an ads SDK. That is followed by 2–4+ months to reach soft-launch depth (meta, IAP store, fail offers, analytics, hundreds of levels).
- **Rough level-count logic, derived from Pixel Flow's engagement:** about 1 h/day across about 10 sessions. Even if difficulty gating (lives/timers) slows progression, engaged players can clear tens of levels per day, so measuring D7–D30 retention credibly needs **hundreds of levels**. Hence the need for a level editor and/or procedural generation with human curation.
- **Engine:** Unity remains the pragmatic default, given publisher SDK/test pipelines and the fast Unity → web ports Poki cites. Godot, Cocos and HTML5 are viable for a web-first strategy. This is an inference only; no engine-share data was found.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures on the typical number of levels at launch or soft launch for jam/sort HC puzzles.
- No sourced € cost of an MVP.
- No data on engine market share among HC-puzzle hits.
- No evidence on the effectiveness of procedural level generation in this subgenre.
- A "6–10-week HC MVP" figure appeared in a snippet with uncertain attribution (a studio services page), so it is unverified.
